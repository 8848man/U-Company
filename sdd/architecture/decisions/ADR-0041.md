# ADR-0041 — Goal squads proposed by the planner, and goal-only external staff from character files

Status: Accepted (2026-10-10, CEO decisions S1–S3 and the external-staff answers recorded in the [v3 plan](../../v3-improvement-plan.md) "2차 개선 — 결정"). Extends [ADR-0038](ADR-0038.md) assignment and refines the [DM-01](../../domain/DM-01-workspace-team.md) busy rule.

## Context

Execution goals look up analysis, planning and review by the fixed IDs `cto`, `pm` and `qa`, so every goal uses the same three characters and nothing records which characters formed the team for a goal. The CEO decided:

- **S1:** a character is reserved only while it works on a stage, not for the whole goal. In addition, the CEO can bring in **external staff**: AI characters made by other people.
- **S2:** the squad is set when the first planner asks the CEO to confirm the plan, which is the project-write approval card.
- **S3:** the planner proposes the squad structure (for example the "출시 준비 팀" preset) and the CEO approves that structure.
- **External staff arrive as character files.** They are contracted for one goal only and leave the roster when that goal completes. The CEO can explicitly hire one permanently.

## Decision

1. **Roster and squad.** The roster stays the existing characters. A squad is an engine-stored record per execution goal (kind `squad`, id = goal id) with:
   - a structure (`standard` or `launch_prep`) and the planner's reason for it;
   - members, each with a slot (`lead`, `writer` or `reviewer`), a duty for leads (`analysis` or `planning`) and the reason they were chosen;
   - a status: `draft` → `proposed` → `locked` → `released`;
   - the approval it was locked under, and the CEO changes made on the approval card.
2. **Leads at start.** When a goal starts, the engine picks one analysis lead (CTO or architecture role, `cto` preferred) and one planning lead (PM or planning role, `pm` preferred). Both must be enabled, have a usable connection, not be busy and not be external staff. The engine does this in the start transaction and saves the squad as `draft`. If no lead is available, the start is refused with the reason, and nothing is called.
3. **Planner proposes the structure.** The planning reply may carry `"squad":{"structure":"standard|launch_prep","reason":"..."}`. A missing or unknown structure becomes `standard`, with that fact recorded as the reason. Structures fix slot counts:

   | Structure | Writers | Reviewers |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | `standard` | up to 2 | 1 |
   | `launch_prep` ("출시 준비 팀", lead 2 · writer 3 · reviewer 2) | up to 3 | up to 2 |

   The engine, not the planner, fills the writer slots from eligible implementers ([ADR-0038](ADR-0038.md) rule: fewest assignments, then roster order) and assigns tasks among those writers only. It fills reviewer slots from enabled review-role characters with a usable connection that are not busy and are not writers of this squad. A shortfall below the structure is allowed only when at least one writer and one reviewer exist, and the shortfall is shown as a fact on the card. Otherwise the plan blocks before approval (`precall`). The squad becomes `proposed`, and the approval text lists the structure, the members and the reasons.
4. **CEO edits only on the approval card (S2).** While the squad is `proposed` and its project-write approval is `pending`, the CEO may swap a writer or a reviewer for another eligible character, including standby external staff. Lead slots are not editable because the leads have already done their work. Each swap runs in one transaction that re-checks:
   - slot role;
   - connection: usable, and able to write files for writers;
   - declared capability against the reassigned tasks for external writers;
   - busy state;
   - that the reviewer is not a writer.

   The CEO may also fill an empty place of the proposed structure: a reviewer, or a writer who takes one task from a writer holding two or more (same checks). A swap or fill reassigns the affected tasks, recomputes the shortfall and regenerates the approval text. The squad has a SHA-256 digest of structure and members. Approving a write approval for a goal that has a squad requires the digest the CEO saw. A mismatch refuses the decision so the CEO reviews again. Approval locks the squad in the same transaction.
5. **Stage reservation (S1).** The busy rule uses the squad:
   - the analysis lead while `analyzing`;
   - the planning lead while `planning`;
   - reviewers while `validating`;
   - writers while their tasks are `pending`, `running` or `awaiting_integration`.

   Goals without a squad keep the old `cto`/`pm`/`qa` reading. Execution, QA, rework planning and resume use only the locked squad's members, and the task claim refuses an assignee that is not a writer of the locked squad. Nobody is substituted silently.
6. **External staff from character files.** A character file is untrusted local JSON (≤ 64 KB) with:
   - `format: "ucompany.character"` and `version: 1`;
   - `name` (≤ 40 characters);
   - optional `title` (≤ 60), `author` (≤ 80) and `capabilities` (`document`, `code`);
   - `slot` (`writer` or `reviewer`);
   - `instructions` (≤ 4,000 characters);
   - optional `appearance` (Office look from the MetroCity catalog, [ADR-0040](ADR-0040.md)); an unknown look is dropped and listed as ignored.

   Any other field is ignored and listed in the preview. That includes connection, executable, args, endpoint, model and keys. Import:
   - never registers an executable or connection, and never sends the file anywhere;
   - binds the character to an existing local connection that the CEO picks;
   - records the file name and SHA-256.

   The imported instructions reach a model only inside the normal task or review prompt of a goal the CEO approved. They are labelled there as untrusted external notes that cannot change the assignment, file scope or safety rules. The app can export a roster character in the same format; the export carries no connection data.
7. **Goal-only contract.**
   - **Standby:** a newly imported external character is on `standby`. The engine never picks it automatically, and it cannot join meetings.
   - **Contracted:** it joins a squad only through a CEO swap on the approval card. Approval moves it to `contracted` for that goal, which makes it busy for every other goal.
   - **Released:** when the goal completes, the squad is `released` and its contracted external staff become `released` and hidden, with their record kept.
   - **Manual end:** the CEO may end a contract by hand while the goal is blocked and no task of that goal is running.
   - **Hiring:** "정식 채용" turns an external character into a regular roster member. It is an explicit CEO action and is never automatic.

## Alternatives

- **Strict reservation for the whole goal:** rejected by the CEO (S1); idle members would block other goals.
- **CEO squad editing at goal submission:** rejected (S2); a goal stays one sentence.
- **Planner naming members directly:** rejected; it cannot check connection, capability or busy state ([ADR-0038](ADR-0038.md)).
- **Online character catalogue:** deferred; it needs a server and an external-transmission approval path.
- **Importing connection settings with a character:** rejected; it would register executables from untrusted files ([Security](../../security.md)).

## Consequences

- Two goals can run analysis, planning or review at the same time when the roster has enough leads and reviewers. With the default five characters, only one goal at a time can be in a lead or review stage.
- The "출시 준비 팀" structure with one QA shows a reviewer shortfall instead of blocking.
- Replacing a locked squad member after approval (for example when a connection breaks) is not part of this decision. Such a goal blocks `precall`, and the CEO repairs the connection.
